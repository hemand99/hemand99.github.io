Current Research

Concept-Level Decision Intelligence using Reinforcement Learning and Large Concept Models (LCM)

Problem Statement

Traditional sentiment-based financial models can detect emotional tone (positive or negative) but fail to capture concept-level reasoning in online discussions. They overlook deeper cognitive elements such as trust, policy interpretation, or risk perception, which are critical for making informed investment decisions.

Research Overview

This study develops a Concept-Level Adaptive Decision System that integrates Reinforcement Learning (RL), the Large Concept Model (LCM), and SONAR semantic embeddings to interpret social discussions (e.g., Reddit posts) beyond surface-level sentiment. Each post is mapped into a high-dimensional conceptual space, where its alignment with decision-relevant ideas—such as investment, risk, trust, and inflation—is measured using cosine similarity.

Through RL, the system dynamically updates Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) weights based on evolving semantic relationships, enabling autonomous, context-aware financial decision outputs such as Invest, Do Not Invest, or Neutral.

Impact

This research bridges the gap between language understanding and decision reasoning, moving beyond text classification toward concept-driven intelligence. It aims to build transparent, self-learning systems capable of generating interpretable, concept-aware insights for financial and behavioral decision-making.

Towards Self-Learning Decision Systems: Combining Reinforcement Learning and Analytic Hierarchy Process (RL + AHP)

This research introduces a self-learning multi-criteria decision framework that integrates Reinforcement Learning (RL) with the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP). Traditional AHP relies on manually assigned weights, but this system allows an RL agent to dynamically update the pairwise comparison matrices based on real-time outcomes. The result is a continuously improving decision model capable of adapting to changing environments—ideal for business analytics, investment ranking, and policy prioritization.

This work highlights a new frontier where classical decision theory meets adaptive learning. This study is currently under review for publication in Springer.
